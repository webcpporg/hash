[#hash-upstream]
== Upstream: Keccak in Boost.Hash2

hash's `keccak_256` exists because Boost.Hash2 has Keccak-256 only under
`detail`: its `detail::keccak_base<0x01, 512, 256>` is exactly the function,
but no public name gives it. This section is the text of a proposal to
Boost.Hash2 that gives it one, with Keccak-224, Keccak-384 and Keccak-512, for
webcpp's owner to open as a pull request. When a Boost release has it,
`webcpp::hash::keccak_256` becomes an alias of
`boost::hash2::keccak_256`, a program no longer links `/third_party//xkcp` for
it, and no result changes, since hash's sponge is already Hash2's
(<<hash-departures-keccak>>).

[#hash-upstream-proposal]
=== The proposal

*Title:* Add keccak_224, keccak_256, keccak_384 and keccak_512.

*Motivation.* Keccak-256, the Keccak team's submission to the SHA-3
competition with its original padding, is the hash of Ethereum and of every
chain and tool compatible with it: addresses, transaction ids, Solidity
selectors, EIP-55 checksums. It differs from SHA3-256 only in its padding
byte, 0x01 instead of FIPS 202's 0x06, and Hash2 already implements both
through `detail::keccak_base`; users who need Keccak-256 today reach into
`detail` or link another library. The four classes below make the existing
code public under the names other libraries use (`Keccak-256` in OpenSSL's
default provider, `keccak_256` in noble-hashes and in Ethereum's tools).

*The change.* In `boost/hash2/sha3.hpp`, after `shake_256`: a class template
`detail::keccak_seeded<D>` over `keccak_base<0x01, 2 * D, D>`, with the three
constructors every SHA-3 class has (the default, the 64-bit seed and the byte
seed, each with the SHA-3 classes' meaning), and four classes that inherit
them, with their HMAC aliases. The seed's eight bytes are fed little-endian,
one at a time, which gives the bytes of `detail::write64le` without an array.

[source]
----
include::{library-root}/test/upstream/keccak.hpp[tag=proposal,indent=0]
----

*The test,* for `test/keccak.cpp`: the digests of the empty message and of the
byte 0xCC are the Len = 0 and Len = 8 entries of the Keccak team's
known-answer files of the SHA-3 competition's third round (`ShortMsgKAT_224.txt` to
`ShortMsgKAT_512.txt`); those of "abc" and the HMAC's were computed with an
independent Keccak that reproduces those entries and Python's SHA-3. It also
checks incremental input and the seeds against `keccak_base` itself.

[source]
----
include::{library-root}/test/upstream_keccak_test.cpp[tag=test,indent=0]
----

*The documentation,* for the reference's list of algorithms: "keccak_224,
keccak_256, keccak_384, keccak_512: Keccak as submitted to the SHA-3
competition, before FIPS 202 changed its padding; Keccak-256 is Ethereum's
hash. Not interchangeable with sha3_224 to sha3_512, whose digests differ."

[#hash-upstream-evidence]
=== The evidence

webcpp builds this text against Boost.Hash2 1.92 in its test
`upstream_keccak`, on every target webcpp has: the digests above pass, and
`webcpp::hash::keccak_256` gives the results of the proposed `keccak_256` for every
length up to three blocks and for random sequences of `update` and `result`,
which is what makes the later alias safe. The proposal is the owner's to open;
nothing here is sent upstream.
